Soft Matter

Multiscale Investigation of Fluid–Solid Interactions in Soft Porous Matter under Indentation

Soft matter, especially soft porous materials, is ubiquitous in the nature. Issues inherent in the response of these materials to external impacts raise problems that are broadly important in diverse areas of science, and increasingly attract attentions in technology. Despite of extensive studies, the focus of this research topic has been placed on the compliant descriptor of the soft matter under indentation. The fluid flow, which plays a critical role in the material’s response to external loading, has been overlooked. Over the past several years, I collaborated with Drs. Sridhar Santhanam and Gang Feng from Villanova University and Dr. Rungun Nathan from Penn State Berks and have developed a systematic, multiscale, technical approach to examine pore fluid flow in a soft porous media during an indentation process, as shown in Figure 15. As compared to the classical Biot theory, the technical approach developed by my group separate the fluid pressurization from the overall reaction force of the porous structure, and has been conclusively verified experimentally. It provides a new perspective to study soft matter, featuring detailed fluid flow and solid densification as a result of impacts on the soft media. The research has been supported by the National Science Foundation. We have published 6 papers on this topic since my tenure and have another two that are currently under review. Two representative papers (Zhu, et al., CES, 2017; Wang, et al., 2019, European J. Mech. B/Fluids) are attached.
