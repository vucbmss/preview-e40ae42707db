Biomimetics-From red cells to soft lubrication

Friction is a major contributor to the energy loss and inefficiencies in mechanical systems. Anywhere that moving parts exist, from large-scale wind turbines to small scale micro-fluidic devices, friction presents to scavenge energy. Therefore, methods to reduce friction is of paramount importance to the safe, reliable and efficient operation of mechanical systems. Through years of evolution, nature has created a model for us to follow to tremendously reduce friction. At the luminal side of the

endothelial cells lies a soft, porous, hydrated endothelial glycocalyx layer (EGL) of glycoproteins andproteoglycans, as shown in Figure 13. An intriguing phenomenon is that, a red blood cell (RBC) can glide over the EGL almost frictionlessly in a tightly fitting capillary. Three competing ideas have been proposed to understand this phenomenon, including the elasto-hydrodynamic theory, the osmotic pressure effect, and the electromagnetic model. However, none of these theories provides a rigorous basis for evaluating their accuracy or limitations since the dimensions of the red cells are obviously too small to obtain detailed measurements, the EGL itself is very fragile and no thicker than one micron, and living cells are contamination prone presenting too many uncontrollable variables. My group is developing a novel biomimetic approach, with the assistance of modern supramolecular chemistry, to examine the super lubrication using highly compressible porous media, as shown in Figure 14. For the first time, we have successfully achieved a reduction of sliding frictional loss by 90.3%,  leading to a completely new research area of soft porous lubrication. The project has been supported by the National Science Foundation for its novelty and originality (quoted from the panel review), of which I am the single principle investigator. We have also published 4 papers on this topic, including the one published in the esteemed Journal of Fluid Mechanics (JFM) and featured on the cover of this journal (Gacka et al., JFM, 2017, attached).
